ROO learned to run

ROO is the fittest of the five built-in creatures after 10,000 generations of the Evolution simulator, ported to run a whole population at once on a GPU. Nobody told it what a gait is; the only thing selected for was how far right it ended up after ten seconds. It covers 204.1 units, 20.41 per second.

The brain that came out

A bias-free feed-forward network, sigmoid on every layer, exactly as FeedForwardNetwork builds it: 6 inputs → 10 hidden → 8 muscles. Each output is mapped to 2·sigmoid-1; negative contracts the muscle, positive expands it, and the magnitude scales the force.

How this was run

  • Ported the game's simulation to a batched 2D XPBD solver in PyTorch and ran it on one Tesla T4. All five built-in body plans evolved in parallel, 512 creatures each, 10,000 generations, 10 simulated seconds per creature at the game's 0.02s fixed timestep.
  • Physics matches the Unity project settings: gravity -50, joint spheres of radius 0.5 with friction 1, the 7 rad/s rigidbody angular cap and the 10 m/s depenetration cap. Muscles pull the bone centers with up to their configured strength (1500 by default).
  • Genetic algorithm as shipped: rank-proportional selection, one-point crossover, global mutation (each gene perturbed by N(0,1) with p=0.75 in a mutating child, mutation rate 0.5) and the best two creatures carried over untouched.
  • Fitness is the game's own running score, (x_end - x_start) / (55 · simulationTime).
  • Because the simulation is chaotic, the champion is not the highest score ever logged - every chromosome of the final population was re-simulated and the creature that actually performs was kept.
  • An earlier run of this had to be thrown away: ground depenetration was feeding real upward momentum back into any joint that punched into the floor, and the population evolved to farm that trampoline instead of running. These numbers are from the re-run after that contact model was fixed.
